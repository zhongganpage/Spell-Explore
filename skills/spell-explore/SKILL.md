# Spell-Explore — core

Adversarial multi-agent proof protocol. rough idea in → attacked, reviewed, and Lean-verified mathematics out. the output is a manuscript at a milestone; nothing is a certificate.

## Roles (all in the background; the Coordinator, the subcoordinators, the PIs, and the lean code runner are resumable — each subcoordinator runs its phase in one run and is resumed by the Coordinator for the next)

- **Coordinator** — asks the user for the rough idea at round 1 and locks it as the goal file; runs each round, enforces the 138-minute timeline (from round 4: 148 minutes), regulates the subcoordinators, measures the system (never the votes), produces a two-consecutive-0/3 steering report (split the goal / nominate pairings / pause), writes the manuscript (PDF) at a milestone, maintains question-routes/. at the start of each round it performs the bounded Formalizer check before the round clock starts: it reads the formalization status line in the Knowledge State index and the live background state, verifies that the four subcoordinators (Creator, Producer, Selector, Formalizer), the PIs, and the lean code runner are present and working — re-spawning any that a resumed session lost — and resolves any stall or conflict it finds; the check is a bounded read that, like the round-1 setup, is not counted in the 138-minute budget (148 minutes from round 4). the Coordinator also keeps the user informed of substantive formalization news as it happens and at each round close: a new green lemma, a new [Formalized] premise, a change in the goal node's distance to the established base, or a significant fragment deposit — routine writes are recorded only in the dossier. its own turns carry the lifecycle contract: every turn ends with a lifecycle line (children-in-flight | awaiting-resume at <window> | round-closed) written to runtime/coordinator-state.md, and it never ends a turn that leaves the round mid-flight undriven (rules/coordinator.md §3). at round start it also creates the clock watcher — one-shot wakes at every binding window end and handoff checkpoint plus a recurring 10-minute backstop (`*/10 * * * *`) that self-arms, so it polls and cuts at every boundary even when its turn ended earlier or a wake was missed, and the atomic close deletes it (rules/timekeeping.md §6); wakes are quiet by default — the current-status table (round clock, phases, live workers, pending artifacts, next action) is shown to the user only when the wake did something real: a boundary cut, a stall repair, a worker completion, or the round close; a no-op wake writes one line to coordinator-state.md and shows nothing (rules/timekeeping.md §6).
- workers are spawned by the Coordinator at a subcoordinator's request — spawn requests at runtime/requests/ in the locked format, workers named by their labels (c/p/s/f-round-type-order), job briefs as file pointers; the swarms are the exception, spawned and regulated by their owners (the Selector's decision swarm of 3, the Formalizer's working swarm of ~4, the lean code runner's swarm of at most 3).
- **Creator** — phase 1: n idea-workers (0 ≤ n ≤ 8, chosen by the Creator per phase; the phases are independent) think around the locked goal (≤10 min) → rotate the n ideas (i → i+1) → summaries (≤10 min) → fresh summaries in the idea pool; phase 2: from round ≥ 2, when the pool has content, 2 graph workers (active when dependency-graph.json has nodes — they write bridging-lemma summaries from the graph) + 2 regular miners in rounds 1–2 (1 + 1 from round 3) mine stale material + the reliable idea set + the fragment region (≤15 min) → rotate → summaries (≤10 min) → fresh summaries; the miners also read the connection marks (the connections section of qmd-index.md / the connection annotations in the single qmd file — the route bridges the Selector's re-invoked promoter writes in the 118–138 window in rounds 1–3 and the 126–148 window from round 4) and are notified by the ids that the route's results or techniques connect the marked statements — they may use ideas from that route; with no pool content the phase produces nothing. from round 2, phase-1 workers must find ideas that do not exist in the pool, and not even similar ones.
- **Producer** — two phases. phase 1: report writers — two in rounds 1–2 and whenever the phase-2 lane is closed, one from round 3 when the lane is open — splitting the fresh summaries as evenly as possible (±1); each writes the idea report (25 min) with its assigned summaries as core, and the hygiene linter then the examine gate it. phase 2: the route writer — gated on (Creator phase 2 on AND accepted routes exist) — revises accepted routes and receives the 2 lowest-goal-frontier leftover summaries. from round 3, when the lane is open, the Producer runs one phase-1 writer (4 fresh summaries arrive per round) and the route writer chooses 0 or 1 summary closest to its accepted route in a 1-minute window at the 20-min mark (round 3 runs 139 minutes, and from round 4 the rounds run 149 minutes, windows shifted +1). a successful report becomes a route with a title, and its writer becomes the PI.
- **Selector** — two panels at a time; each route gets a panel (workerA evidence list by 78 — 83 from round 4, B/C/D review 63–93 — 63–98 from round 4, exchange 93–103 — 98–108 from round 4 → 3 review summaries, each carrying the reviewer's questions for the PI), a canary gate, the PI rebuttal + promoter note (103–118 — the PI answers every question; the answer quality weighs in the BCD vote; from round 4: 108–126), then the swarm of 3 (odd) + the BCD vote — B/C resumed, workerD re-invoked externally (118–138, 20 min in rounds 1–3; 126–148, 22 min from round 4), plus the re-invoked promoter's connection marking in the same window — it reads the revised route, the change list and the BCD review reports, inspects the single qmd file, and marks every statement-pair the route's results or techniques bridge (`[<route title>-T-<implied id>]` on the initial statement, `[<route title>-F-<initial id>]` on the implied statement, as qmd annotation lines — a REQUIRED close deliverable the Selector verifies by file before it closes, never silently dropped (rules/selector.md §7.1, §11)). verdicts accept / accept-core (the salvageable core is banked as a reduced route with its own title) / reject, each rejecting vote naming the load-bearing obstruction. the reviewers and swarm judge the route itself; the promoter's note serves as a high-level check. acceptance = ≥2/3 swarm AND ≥2/3 BCD; milestone = 3/3 + 3/3 + the accepted routes achieving the locked goal — operationally, the goal node reachable from the established base (kernel axioms + Mathlib theorems + [Formalized] pieces), `#print axioms goalTheorem` containing no non-kernel axiom, and a full manuscript claim requiring the hired set empty and the reachability proven in Lean.
- **Formalizer** (mandated) — decompose workers (per pair, 10-min limit, 10-min unpaired timeout), inputs are verdict-aware (examine-failed reports immediately; accepted/accepted-core routes + the promoter's note and connection report post-verdict from the Selector) → decomposed fragments → mechanical swarm of ~4 (a job running >10 min is packaged and relayed to a swarm worker that has done its job; 1-min wait, else the fragment region) → single qmd file → lean code runner (plans the verification jobs in advance, dispatches them to its own swarm of the required number (at most 3) — unrelated to the working swarm — and integrates: locks green pieces, builds the dependency tree: statement nodes (kernel | mathlib | formalized | axiom | goal) + green edges + [Hired] + the goal node) → reliable idea set (green lean only, [Formalized], un-overturnable) + fragment region. runs across rounds, unbound by the round budget. it keeps the formalization status in the Knowledge State index current: after each completed batch — a decompose run, a swarm batch that writes its per-fragment files, or a lean code runner, resumed once per round at the round start (run-or-postpone gate), greening a piece or depositing fragments — it writes one dated line recording the green count, the [Formalized] count, the fragment deposits and the dependency graph delta, so the index always reflects the current formalization state and the Coordinator's round-start check reads a live number.

## The 138-minute round (one route) — from round 4 extended: 148-minute base, 149 min with the Producer's +1 (the Producer's 1-minute summary choice adds at the 20-min mark from round 3)

0–20 Creator phase 1 · 20–45 Producer report (25) · 45–63 linter (layer 1 ≈3, layer 2 produces the format ≈7) + examine (cap 8) · 63–103 / 63–108 Selector panel · 103–118 / 108–126 PI rebuts + promoter (parallel) · 118–138 / 126–148 swarm + resumed BCD + the re-invoked promoter's connection marking (rounds 1–3 / from round 4). phases that overrun are cut; the round closes atomically at 138 min in rounds 1–2, 139 min in round 3, and 149 min from round 4. the round-1 setup (asking for the rough idea → writing the goal file) happens before the clock starts.

## Invariants

- every Agent call — spawn AND resume-by-ID — runs in the explicit background mode (`run_in_background=true`), never the blocking foreground. A resume is never foreground. AgentSwarm has no `run_in_background` flag (tool limitation); swarm workers run asynchronously, and the lifecycle contract handles the block via children-in-flight yield
- a subcoordinator never returns early — its final message comes only after every worker it directs has produced its artifact at the assigned path and it has verified the write; a narrated step is not a done step
- the Coordinator's own turns carry the same contract: every turn ends with a lifecycle line (children-in-flight | awaiting-resume at <window> | round-closed) written to runtime/coordinator-state.md, and a turn that ends mid-round without one is an early return — the round resumes from that file, never from task-state inference
- no subcoordinator closes a worker that a later stage still needs (see rules/worker-lifespans.md)
- the idea pool is append-only for workers; nothing in it may be deleted by a worker
- everything is versioned (the goal file excepted — it is locked and never changed by the project)
- artifacts are files: every worker writes to its assigned output path and confirms; a worker that cannot write has its text persisted verbatim, marked recovered from agent output
- the votes are fixed — the Coordinator never affects the acceptance thresholds
- accepted routes are revisable, not the end of a route; only a demonstrated counterexample kills an idea
- the Formalizer keeps the Knowledge State index's formalization status current, and the Coordinator reads it at the round-start check

## Running it

1. **Start:** run the skill's `scripts/init-project.sh` (ships with this skill, next to `references/`) to scaffold the project — copies the agent profiles, the dossier skeleton, offers the config merge — then start Kimi Code in that directory.
2. **Round 1:** the Coordinator asks the user for the rough idea, writes the locked goal file, the user picks the round count — the binding cap, recorded in runtime/coordinator-state.md and the dossier; the rounds auto-run to it — and the Coordinator asks whether to add an exterior reviewer X for the panel's workerD — configured once as `X_PROVIDER` / `X_MODEL` / `X_ACCESS` (a provider API env var or the local Codex CLI; see `references/protocol/modules/providers.md`), or none (internal D) — and helps configure it. it also asks the source-repository path (protocol-src — the local clone of the Spell-Explore repository holding the canonical protocol; used for the protocol-integrity check at each begin/resume) and the runner-mode: auto-run the lean code runner at each round start from round 2 (resumed automatically whenever pending lean work exists), or ask run-or-postpone each time (manual — the default).
3. **Each round:** check the Formalizer's status at round start (bounded read before the clock; re-spawn the four subcoordinators (Creator, Producer, Selector, Formalizer), the PIs, and the lean code runner if a resumed session lost any); run the protocol-integrity check against the source repo (protocol-src) and align with it when they differ (the repo's `scripts/check-skill.sh` + `scripts/sync-skill.sh`); follow the timeline; use the templates in `references/protocol/templates/` for every artifact; enforce each subcoordinator's rules; act on any stale signal from the accepted-route watch so the demotion completes by round close; close atomically with the decision list (abstracts of accepted routes, recycle/park for each unaccepted route, user nominations for next-round pairings).
4. **Continue:** rounds ≥ 2 resume carried-over reviews first, run the Creator alongside; the rounds auto-run — the Coordinator starts the next round at each round close until the round count chosen at round 1 is reached, stopping early only at a milestone (the Coordinator writes the manuscript PDF), on the two-consecutive-0/3 steering stop, or when the user says stop. the user's per-close decisions (seeing accepted routes, recycle/park, pairings) never block the next round's start — unaccepted routes default to park until the user decides. at each close with rounds remaining the Coordinator asks you to run `/compact` with its instruction (rules/timekeeping.md §7) — non-blocking, and it keeps later rounds cheaper by re-reading a bounded context instead of the whole conversation. the subcoordinators are bounded the same way across round boundaries: each closes at the round close and is spawned fresh at the next round from its resume pack (rules/coordinator.md §3, the round-boundary bounded context), so the pipeline's contexts stop growing across rounds.

expectations: early rounds may deliver zero accepted routes — the unit of progress is a dossier entry, not a route; routes are rare successes. the secondary-model worker tier (idea workers, miners, graph workers, report workers, route workers, swarm workers, decompose workers, lean-swarm workers, workerA) needs `KIMI_CODE_EXPERIMENTAL_SECONDARY_MODEL=1` exported before kimi starts, and the protocol config (`[subagent] timeout_ms = 0`) is applied at the same start — the round-start preflight verifies both and surfaces the restart caveat when either is not in effect. the exterior reviewer X (workerD's external path) needs no experimental flag: it is configured once at round 1 and invoked through the provider API or the local Codex CLI (`references/protocol/modules/providers.md`); the Coordinator verifies its provider family differs from the primary's at round 1 and re-checks the access at each round start.
